Five calculators that answer the questions a screenshot cannot.

No signup, no upload, no server round trip. Change a number and the answer updates. These are planning tools, not forecasts — the assumptions are stated under each one.

1. Position size from risk

How many lots put a fixed percentage of the balance at risk at a given stop distance.

Lots = (balance × risk%) ÷ (stop in pips × value per pip per lot). For XAUUSD, confirm the contract size and the broker’s pip definition — a “pip” is not the same on every symbol.

2. What spread and commission cost you

The annual drag of trading costs, before any strategy edge.

Cost per trade = lots × (commission + spread × value per pip). This is the cost you must overcome before the strategy makes a cent. Swap is not included; add it if you hold overnight.

3. Walk-forward efficiency

Out-of-sample profit divided by in-sample profit, for the same selected parameters.

Above 0.5 is worth a second look; between 0 and 0.5 is weak; at or below 0 the selection did not generalise. If the in-sample result is not positive, the ratio is undefined — quote the out-of-sample number instead.

4. Drawdown recovery

The gain required to get back to where you were. It is always larger than the loss.

Required gain = drawdown ÷ (1 − drawdown). A 50% loss needs a 100% gain; a 30% loss needs 42.9%. This is why risk management is not optional.

5. Prop-firm challenge pass probability

A simple Monte Carlo of independent trades. It ignores commissions, slippage and correlation — treat it as a sanity check, not a promise.

Press the button to run the simulation.

Each trade risks the chosen percentage of current equity and wins or loses with the given probability. Pass if equity reaches the target before it breaches the drawdown limit. Fail by drawdown, or by running out of trades. No commissions, no slippage, no serial correlation.
