Your EA does what the code says, not what you meant.

An audit reads the code against the stated rules, finds the places where they differ, and shows you the difference in a test. It is not a rewrite and it is not a profit forecast.

What an audit covers

  • Lot sizing. Risk percentage against stop distance, tick value vs point value, contract size, and the rounding that silently doubles or halves the risk.
  • Stops and targets. Whether the stop is on the position or only in the code, whether it survives a restart, and what happens on a gap.
  • Magic numbers and position selection. Whether the EA can see another EA’s trades, and whether it closes the right one.
  • Session and time. Broker server time vs your time, daylight saving, Friday close, and the news filter that fires an hour late.
  • Fills and slippage. Market vs limit, partial fills, requotes, and the assumption that an order fills at the price on the chart.
  • History and backtest assumptions. Modelling mode, spread, commission, swap, and the difference between “every tick” and real ticks.
  • Failure paths. What happens when the terminal disconnects, the order is rejected, the array is short, or the indicator handle is invalid.

What it is not

  • It is not a guarantee of profit, and it is not a strategy review in the “make it better” sense.
  • It is not a rewrite. If the architecture is wrong, the report says that and quotes a rebuild separately.
  • It is not a substitute for a walk-forward. A bug-free EA can still have no edge; that is a validation, and it is a different question.
