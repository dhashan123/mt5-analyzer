The 5-point backtest checklist.

Print it, paste it into your notes, or send it to whoever sold you the EA. If any answer is “I don’t know”, that is the part worth fixing first.

  1. Was every run pinned to the same configuration?
    Symbol, timeframe, spread modelling, date range, deposit, leverage and currency. If two runs differ in any of these, the comparison is not a comparison.
  2. Were the parameters chosen on data the test never saw?
    A parameter sweep ranked by profit selects the setting that fitted the sample best. Only a walk-forward — choose on one window, run on the next unseen one — answers whether it generalises.
  3. Does it survive commission and swap at 2× and 3×?
    A strategy that only works at today’s costs is not a strategy; it is a bet on your broker’s pricing.
  4. Is the profit concentrated in one week, one hour, or one symbol?
    Rebuild the equity curve from the deals and split the profit by day and hour. Concentration is the fingerprint of a curve fit.
  5. What is the worst losing streak, and where in the sample did it happen?
    A 12-loss run in the final month is a different risk from one spread evenly across three years. The location matters as much as the length.
One more question that is not on the list. Who is telling you this, and what do they lose if you say no? A report that only ever confirms what you hoped is worth nothing. The sample report on this site is a failure, published in full — that is the standard.
