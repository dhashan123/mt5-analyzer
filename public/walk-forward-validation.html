Walk-forward validation, explained with a failure.

A backtest tells you what happened on data you chose. A walk-forward tells you what happened on data the parameters never saw. Only one of those is evidence.

The problem with one run

A Strategy Tester report is the result of one run, on one symbol, over one date range, with one set of parameters. If those parameters were chosen by looking at the result — or by trying several until the curve looked good — the report is a description of the search, not a property of the strategy.

This is not a subtle statistical point. It is the most common way a backtest becomes a curve fit: rank a parameter grid by net profit, pick the top cell, and present it as the strategy. The top cell is, by construction, the one that fitted that sample best. The question is whether it still works on the next sample.

What walk-forward does

  1. Split the sample into consecutive windows: a training window and the test window immediately after it.
  2. On the training window, run a parameter sweep and pick the best cell by a declared objective — expectancy, not net profit.
  3. Take those parameters and run them, unchanged, on the test window the selection never saw.
  4. Roll forward and repeat. Stitch the out-of-sample results into one curve.

The number that matters is walk-forward efficiency: out-of-sample profit divided by in-sample profit for the same parameters. Near 1.0 means the selection generalised. Near 0 means the in-sample result was mostly curve fitting. Negative means the selection actively hurt.

What a report should contain

  • Every run pinned to one configuration: symbol, timeframe, spread modelling, dates, deposit, leverage, currency.
  • The sweep table, ranked by the declared objective, with the failures left visible.
  • Each fold: the selected parameters, the in-sample result, and the out-of-sample result side by side.
  • The efficiency number, the profitable-fold ratio, and a plain reading of both.
  • Cost stress at 2× and 3× commission and swap.
  • The worst losing streak, where it happened, and the equity curve rebuilt from the deals.
  • The raw terminal report and the exact commands to reproduce every run.

What it cannot tell you

It cannot tell you the future. It cannot tell you that a strategy will make money. It can tell you whether the evidence you have is evidence, or a curve fitted to the past. That is a narrower claim, and it is the only one that survives contact with a live account.

No profit claims. Every report is a historical simulation with modelled execution. The deliverable is measurement, including a negative result. If the walk-forward says the edge did not survive, the report says so.
