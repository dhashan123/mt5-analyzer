Walk-forward validation

A backtest tells you what happened. A walk-forward test tells you what is likely to happen next, and it is the only one of the two that is worth paying for.

The whole service is one question, answered honestly: does your strategy still work on data its parameters never saw? The parameters are chosen on one window, run on the next, unseen one, and the out-of-sample result is reported as measured — including when it says your edge did not survive.

No profitability claims. Not here, not in any report, not in any message. The deliverable is measurement. A report that only ever confirms what you hoped is worth nothing, and you should refuse to pay for one.

How it works

  1. Send your EA. .mq5 source is best (with any .mqh it needs); .ex5 is fine. Plus the symbol, timeframe, date range and deposit you want, or say "you choose" and I will pick something sensible and tell you what I picked.
  2. It is run. Every run is pinned to one configuration — symbol, timeframe, spread modelling, dates, deposit, leverage, currency — so the runs are actually comparable. Modelling mode is every tick based on real ticks by default, because it is the only mode that shows what a stop would really have done inside the bar.
  3. You get the report. A self-contained HTML file you can open anywhere, the CSVs behind it, the raw terminal report, and the exact commands to reproduce every run on your own broker.

What I will not do

  • Tune your parameters until the numbers look good, and call that a validation.
  • Present an in-sample result as if it were out-of-sample.
  • Quote a number I have not reproduced myself.
  • Promise that your strategy is profitable. I do not know that, and neither does any other person who tells you they do.
